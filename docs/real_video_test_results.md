# Milestone 11A — Independent real-video results

The five manual counters each completed at least one cycle on new footage. Manual selection produced 104 cycles across 18 clips. Automatic selection produced 53, of which 52 were push-ups. Both modes processed the same 10,233 source frames to end of file, with zero processing failures. The 51-cycle difference measures the effect of changing recognition mode on these recordings. Independent repetition counts and protocol-compatible per-repetition quality labels were unavailable, so counting and form accuracy remain unestablished.

## Sources and experimental design

All five requested Pexels downloads decoded. We inspected frames at multiple times for movement, framing, viewpoint and equipment. Four entered the experiment as natural-view tests. The proposed pull-up shows a muscle-up ending in straight-arm support above the bar. We excluded it and evaluated three fixed-camera pull-up recordings, including a rear-view chin-visibility control. Two additional Pexels twists and a frontal timed Navy challenge let us distinguish failures before and after torso-rotation sampling. We kept complete clips, including transitions, starts, dismounts and overlays. [Rejected muscle-up](https://www.pexels.com/video/a-man-lifting-himself-over-the-pull-up-bar-4804760/) · [Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/)

The Mendeley sample comprises the first two subject IDs (001 and 002) in each side-view Squat and Push-up Good/Bad group. Eight ZIP members were selectively retrieved using HTTP byte ranges against the exercise-quality archive. Each extracted file matched the member size and CRC32. The labels describe the source clip as a whole. The gym footage includes background people, face blurring and occasional occlusion. The sources supplied neither a count nor the mapping from each repetition to a human verdict under this application's protocol. The Pexels and DVIDS clips supplied no such annotations either. The 60-second timer on the Navy challenge measures time. All human count, human quality and count-error fields are empty. The previously used development squat was excluded from these measurements.

Dataset authors are Duddela Sai Prashanth, Priya Kamath, Rakshith Bhandary, S Ashlesh Pai, Pratham PN, Advith P and Abhishek Mashetty (2026), *A Multi-View Raw Video Dataset of Seven Fitness Exercises with Good/Bad Form Labels*, version 3. The DVIDS challenge is a released U.S. Navy video by Mass Communication Specialist 2nd Class Robert S. Price, marked public domain by DVIDS. [Mendeley dataset and DOI](https://doi.org/10.17632/kgbb3yn47p.3) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) · [DVIDS challenge](https://www.dvidshub.net/video/757656/one-minute-russian-twist-challenge) · [DVIDS usage terms](https://www.dvidshub.net/about/copyright)

The inventory records all 55 downloaded candidate files, the 18 selected, each source page, media URL or archive member, credit, license, and the view or reason for exclusion. The results include the SHA-256 of every evaluated MP4. The optional [Marines video index](https://www.fitness.marines.mil/videolinks/) returned HTTP 403. The DVIDS source pages and their linked MP4s were accessible directly. Sampling was purposive. Some clips deliberately challenge camera placement, and subjects and sites repeat. Independence here means these are new to the project's documented real-video checks. Participant independence from the original frame-training dataset could not be verified. ## What failed, and where

**Squat.** On the four Mendeley side views, manual counting yielded 14, 6, 3 and 7 cycles. Automatic counting yielded zero on every one. Unknown ranged from 61.5% to 88.3%. Subject 001 Good lost pose tracking on 136/479 frames, and 233 frames lacked a usable classifier input. On subject 002 Good, only 2/467 frames lacked input, yet 227 were rejected for low score. Pose loss alone cannot explain the automatic result. Across the five manual runs, depth passed/failed/was unavailable on 5/4/22 cycles, standing extension on 14/0/17, and upright endpoints on 6/8/17. The single frontal stock-footage cycle had insufficient side-view coverage in both modes.

**Push-up.** All four Mendeley clips produced identical counts and verdict totals in both modes, with zero wrong active frames. On subject 002 Bad the system called 9 of 13 cycles GOOD. On subject 001 Good, it called 6 of 9 BAD, each with failed elbow flexion. These disagreements with the *clip* labels identify cases for review but supply no per-rep error rate. Across the 52 manual cycles, extension passed on all 52. Flexion passed/failed/was unavailable on 31/10/11. Alignment passed/failed on 48/4, extended-leg posture on 38/14 and knee clearance on 46/6. The oblique outdoor stock clip lacked the required five-angle classifier input on all 291 frames. Manual mode also returned zero despite visible push-up motion and reported unreliable required joints on 107 frames.

**Pull-up.** Frames at the start, during the movement and later in each clip were inspected at source resolution. Calibration used the centerline of the *gripped* bar at the athlete's face column, measured from the image top. Pexels 31622705 used approximately 83/720 = 0.115278 (lower of two bars in a low frontal projection). Pexels 14293451 used 335/720 = 0.465278 (oblique, near image x=480). Pexels 39954722 used 535/1280 = 0.417969 (near-horizontal foreground bar, rear view). The manifest retains full precision. A single calibrated height is a local approximation on a sloped bar. The first clip also shows leg swing.

Manual mode counted 3, 1 and 5 cycles respectively. The optional Face Landmarker was enabled on all 2,318 frames in each mode, with **zero associated chin observations**. Neither clearance nor the below-bar hang was observable on any completed cycle. Swing evidence was unavailable on all nine, and eight lacked extension evidence. One passed extension. Automatic mode completed zero cycles despite the raw classifier selecting Pull-up on 676/1,098, 493/618 and 479/602 frames. The rear control hides the face. The two forward-facing views also lack usable chin evidence, and the available telemetry does not distinguish face detection from failed pose–face association.

**Jumping Jack.** The raw classifier returned Jumping Jack on 279/576 frames and Push-up on 263. Temporal selection left 360 frames Unknown and selected Push-up on 73. Manual mode completed 10 cycles while automatic mode completed zero. All ten closed-stance endpoints passed. Open width passed twice and was unavailable eight times. Coordination passed five times and was unavailable five times. Overhead reach and simultaneous opening were unavailable on all ten. The manual counter issued 127 front-view and 12 unreliable-joint warnings. These support a view/coverage limitation. They cannot establish whether the athlete's form was incorrect.

**Russian Twist.** On Pexels 6892537, both shoulders and both hips had world landmarks on all 284 frames, yet manual selection stayed in “Find start” throughout. It reported 114 front-view, 57 hands-together and 111 unreliable-joint warnings. On 10315299 manual mode likewise stayed in “Find start” for all 564 frames. On 6455345 it reached “First side”, invoked the real world-landmark sampler 124 times and obtained 21 right-turn samples. It never completed the opposite side. Seated support was unavailable on all 124 calls.

The frontal DVIDS challenge exercised both directions end to end. Manual mode called the sampler 85 times, obtaining 42 right-turn and 28 left-turn values. It had seated-support evidence on 38 calls. Two cycles completed. Both passed hand-center and torso-center return. Start lean passed once and failed once. Bilateral reach, peak rotation and support lacked sufficient cycle evidence on both, so both verdicts were UNASSESSABLE. Automatic mode was Unknown on 1,476/1,799 frames. It sampled the twist logic 11 times and completed zero cycles. The source includes graphics and transitions, raised feet and intermittent visibility. An available world-coordinate sample alone does not establish a sustained turn or supported posture.

## Reproduction and verification

Starting pipeline revision was `abacba1d2574035806e9d6a25a0b9b8fb44cf29e` (merged PR #12). Each run used a fresh session, CPU Pose Landmarker Lite, the committed Random Forest, the existing classifier gates, counter and form protocols. Face Landmarker ran on calibrated pull-up streams. No exercise thresholds or model weights changed. Timings include decoding, model/session initialization, CPU inference, the read-only observation callbacks and session reporting, with the HUD disabled. The replays ran sequentially on a shared x86-64 Debian 13 cloud worker, Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C, three visible CPUs, two CPUs of cgroup quota. Rates ranged from 26.7 to 73.2 FPS. No physical webcam, target Ubuntu laptop or display was tested.

Python 3.12.14, MediaPipe 0.10.32, OpenCV contrib 4.11.0.86, NumPy 1.26.4 and scikit-learn 1.6.1 were installed. Pose asset SHA-256 was `59929e1d1ee95287735ddd833b19cf4ac46d29bc7afddbbf6753c459690d574a`, face asset `64184e229b263107bc2b804c6625db1341ff2bb731874b0bcc2fe6544e0bc9ff`, and RF `e2f71f317c5988ff8f0a2e306abf649817cc69b79c8e51ae16d4d523aaa173b6`.

After the normal project setup, place the selected source files in one local directory with their exact filenames. From the repository root, copy the manifest alongside them and replay. The supplemental observer invokes the same validation runner and records the actual model outputs, phases, rule outcomes and sample availability. Plain `python validate_videos.py` accepts the same manifest.

```sh
cp docs/experiments/real-video-11a/manifest.csv /path/to/local/clips/manifest.csv
.venv/bin/python -m scripts.observe_validation /path/to/local/clips/manifest.csv --output sessions/validation
```

All 36 pairs of source/processed frame counts matched. Both modes had identical raw prediction histograms on each clip. We checked every session CSV against cycle and quality totals, including equality of completed cycles and verdicts. A separate two-mode rerun of clip 6455345 matched the archived supplementary observations using the published observer. The CSV retains full-precision rates, per-label wrong counts, warnings and missing-rule totals. The JSON adds pose/chin coverage, raw and active histograms, phase occupancy, twist samples, cycle times, and satisfied/failed/unavailable rule names. Warning types can overlap on a frame. The entire regression suite passed all **86 tests in 2.286 seconds**, with `POSE_TEST_VIDEO` set to the separate development squat for the opt-in real-pose notebook parity test. We ran `POSE_TEST_VIDEO=/tmp/iea-squat.mp4 .venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tests -v`.

## Next engineering milestone

1. Have an independent reviewer mark start, bottom/top and completion times on the four Mendeley squat clips and the jack clip. Align raw predictions, temporal decisions, joint availability and counter resets to those times. This can distinguish loss of classifier input from switching labels during a valid cycle.
2. Have a qualified reviewer label individual push-ups against the fixed protocol, starting with 001 Good and 002 Bad. Compare elbow depth, knee posture and alignment at the same timestamps before revising a form rule.
3. Capture a stationary side/oblique pull-up with a level bar, visible near-side joints and enough face detail at both hang and peak. Record face detection and association separately. For twists, collect level frontal, feet-supported recordings with hands together, both wrists and ankles visible. For jacks, leave overhead framing margin and keep the feet visible. Recheck rule coverage before tuning any thresholds.
