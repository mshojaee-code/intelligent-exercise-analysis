# Exercise form references and observable rules

The exercise libraries define the movements. This project selects one variant per exercise and measures a subset of its requirements from a fixed camera. **All numeric pass/fail limits below are provisional engineering tolerances.** NASM's approximate 90° push-up depth cue is identified separately. A GOOD verdict describes the observed protocol criteria for one completed attempt.

| Variant and reference section | What the reference asks the participant to do | What the application checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Parallel bodyweight squat.** American Council on Exercise (ACE), *Bodyweight Squat*, Steps 1–6 | Start standing, bend the hips and knees, lower toward parallel, keep the feet stable and rise under control. | In a level side view, the start and finish require a knee angle ≥160° with shoulder, hip, knee and ankle ordered top to bottom. The knee excursion identifies an attempt. The signed hip-to-knee thigh slope measures its depth. A sustained slope ≤10° from horizontal includes parallel and deeper positions. Heel travel >0.15 torso lengths gives an optional stability cue. Trunk lean is recorded. Side view cannot establish frontal knee alignment, muscle bracing or foot pressure. [ACE exercise](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/) |
| **Standard full push-up.** National Academy of Sports Medicine (NASM), *Push-Up*, “How to Perform a Push-Up,” “Common Mistakes” and the depth FAQ | Hold a straight plank, lower the chest near the floor or bend the elbows to approximately 90°, then extend the arms. Avoid sagging or raised hips. Knee support is a modification. | From a full-body side view, use elbow ≤100° at depth and ≥155° at both ends. Shoulder–hip–ankle ≥165° measures body alignment. Hip position relative to the shoulder–ankle line distinguishes sag from pike. Leg extension ≥150° and knee clearance ≥0.10 torso lengths above the wrist–toe line give evidence of a full plank. Head alignment, chest–floor distance and load on the toes are outside the measurement. [NASM exercise](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/push-up) |
| **Strict pull-up.** NASM, *Pull-Up*, “How to Perform a Pull-Up” and “Common Mistakes” | Hang with extended arms, pull upward until the chin clears the bar, lower to a hang and avoid excessive swing. The project selects NASM's chin-clearance option. | Use a fixed near-side view showing the bar, face and arm. Require elbow ≥150° at both ends. Compare the lower midline chin on the face oval (vertex 152) with the calibrated top edge of the bar. Require a clear hang below at each end and two consecutive upper-phase frames above, with a 0.01-image-height margin. Sustained horizontal hip travel >0.50 torso lengths flags visible swing. Projection, face occlusion and camera movement limit the comparison. [NASM exercise](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/pull-up) |
| **Classic jumping jack.** NASM, *Jumping Jacks*, “How to Perform Jumping Jacks” | Start with feet together and arms down, open the legs while both arms rise overhead, then close together. | From the front, check both wrists against a conservative crown estimate above the eyes. At peak, require both arms overhead simultaneously with ankle span ≥2.0 projected hip widths. Finish at ≤1.4 widths with both wrists down. At most 35% of observed frames may have mismatched arm/leg opening. The 2.0 ratio separates full opening from the existing 1.7 attempt gate and scales with body size. NASM describes the stance qualitatively. Body proportions and head tilt can bias these proxies. [NASM exercise](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/jumping-jacks) |
| **Seated Russian twist, planted feet.** NASM, *Russian Twist*, “How to Perform a Russian Twist” and “Common Mistakes” | Sit with bent knees, lean slightly back, hold the hands together and lead the turn with the shoulders through center to both sides. This project selects NASM's feet-planted option. | From the front, the hand path counts center → side → opposite side → center. Relative to the starting pose, require ≥15° of shoulder turn in each reach direction, both in the camera frame and relative to the pelvis. Smooth relative yaw with EMA (α=0.5), confirm it on adjacent frames and require return within 10° of neutral. Check initial lean, bent knees and stable ankles. Estimated monocular depth approximates rotation. Ankle stability cannot prove floor contact, and the model cannot establish which muscles initiated the turn. [NASM exercise](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/russian-twist) |

## From observations to a verdict

The counter emits one event on a completed return. The assessor accumulates evidence over that cycle. A shallow squat or push-up can therefore count once and receive BAD. An unfinished attempt receives no verdict.

- **GOOD** requires every essential observable criterion to pass.
- **BAD** requires a clear essential violation with sufficient evidence. The assessor records the failed rule, for example insufficient thigh depth or a clearly sub-bar chin throughout the upper phase.
- **UNASSESSABLE** records an attempt with insufficient evidence. A missing or ambiguous chin/bar comparison or unreliable rotation or seated-base measurement prevents a strict verdict for that protocol. Optional cues have no effect on the quality total.

The assessor requires at least five usable frames and 75% coverage. Peak tests use adjacent samples. The classifier continues to use its original raw five-angle schema. [Detailed geometry, units and camera setup](fixed_exercise_protocols.md) · [Evidence aggregation](form_assessment.md)
